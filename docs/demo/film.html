The problem

An agent that never trades isn't disciplined. It's broken.

Give a language model a brokerage API and it will find a reason to trade. Put a filter in front of it and it will find a reason to never trade. Both fail — and from the outside, both look identical.

The differentiator

We re-price every trade we refused.

Refusals graded4re-priced at current marks
Refusals correct0avoided a loss
Refusals costly4missed a profit
Hit rate0%our filter was wrong

Every refusal our gate made would have been profitable. We published that unchanged, and lowered the floor from 0.15 to 0.12 on the evidence.

And we refused to over-claim

The sweep says “trade everything.” We didn't listen.

Every re-priced spread is a winner, because SPY rose all week. With no loser in the sample the curve can only fall, and its maximum is an artefact of one rally — not a property of the filter. A floor exists to cap the loser that hasn't happened yet.

*** THE SAMPLE CANNOT DISCRIMINATE *** All 9 re-priced spreads are winners. The shipped floor stays at 0.12; this frontier is not evidence for lowering it further.
Built against the real API

Three undocumented Alpaca behaviours, found by running it.

  • Multi-leg limit sign is inverted. Sent +0.15, filled −0.13 — a positive limit authorises paying a debit for a credit spread.
  • Greeks need --feed indicative. The default feed 403s; the snapshots endpoint returns nulls and silently kills delta targeting.
  • Intraday portfolio history is wrong in paper. Reports exactly $100,000 too high. It would have put an equity chart showing our account doubling on the submission page.
